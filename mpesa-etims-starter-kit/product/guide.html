1. Why records matter now

Many small businesses in Kenya still keep records in a notebook, or not at all. Sales come in through M-Pesa, cash, WhatsApp orders and credit to regular customers, and by month end nobody knows the real profit.

From 1 January 2026, KRA has said it will check the income and expenses people declare in their tax returns against electronic data, including eTIMS invoices, withholding tax records and customs records. In practice:

  • A business expense without a valid eTIMS invoice may not be accepted as a cost when KRA works out your income tax. If the cost is rejected, your taxable profit goes up.
  • Your sales may be compared with other data, such as what your customers claim as purchases from you.
  • Good records protect you if KRA asks questions. They also show you which products actually make money.
The key habit: for every business purchase, ask for an eTIMS invoice with your KRA PIN on it. The tracker turns an expense red if you don't have one.

Records also help you grow

  • Know your true profit, not just the money in your M-Pesa.
  • See which customers owe you, and follow up.
  • Show clean records when you apply for a loan, a tender or a supplier credit line.

2. eTIMS in plain language

eTIMS (electronic Tax Invoice Management System) is KRA's system for creating tax invoices electronically. Each eTIMS invoice is recorded by KRA, so both you and your customer have proof of the transaction.

QuestionSimple answer
Who is it for?KRA says eTIMS applies to people carrying on business, whether or not they are registered for VAT. There is a simpler version, eTIMS Lite, made for small and micro businesses that are not VAT registered.
Does it cost money?KRA's own eTIMS options (eCitizen web, *222# USSD and the eTIMS app) are free to use. Paid software that connects to eTIMS is optional.
Do I issue one for every sale?eTIMS invoices are meant for your business sales. Customers who are businesses will ask for them, because they need them to claim the cost. Confirm with KRA whether you must issue one for every small cash sale.
What if my supplier is very small?KRA rules allow a buyer to issue a tax invoice on behalf of certain small suppliers (a "reverse invoice"). Ask KRA or a tax professional whether this applies to you.
Rules and thresholds are reported differently by different sources. When in doubt, the official KRA eTIMS page (kra.go.ke → eTIMS) is the final word.

3. Getting onto eTIMS: three free options

Before you start, have your KRA PIN, your iTax password (or eCitizen account) and the phone number linked to your KRA PIN.

OptionBest forHow to start
eTIMS Lite: web (through eCitizen)Using a smartphone browser or computer Go to the KRA eTIMS page on kra.go.ke, choose the eTIMS Lite / eCitizen option, sign in and follow the onboarding steps.
eTIMS Lite: USSD *222#Any phone, no internet. For individuals and sole proprietors. Dial *222# from the SIM linked to your KRA PIN and follow the menu prompts to register and create invoices.
eTIMS app (for non-VAT businesses)Smartphone users who issue many invoices Download KRA's official eTIMS app for non-VAT taxpayers from the Play Store and sign in with your KRA details.
Tip: Menus on *222# and the app can change, so read each screen carefully rather than memorising numbers. If you get stuck, visit a Huduma Centre or KRA service desk, or call the KRA contact centre listed on kra.go.ke.
Watch out for scams. KRA does not ask for your password or PIN by SMS or WhatsApp. Only use official KRA channels and apps.

After you are onboarded

  1. Add the items or services you sell (name, price) so invoicing is quick.
  2. Make one test invoice for a small sale so you learn the steps.
  3. Write the eTIMS invoice number in column L of the Sales sheet in your tracker.

4. Issuing an eTIMS invoice: step by step

  1. Get the customer's details. For a business customer, ask for their KRA PIN and name. "Naomba KRA PIN yako ili nikutumie invoice ya eTIMS."
  2. Open eTIMS (web, *222# or app) and choose to create a sales invoice.
  3. Enter the customer's PIN, the item(s), quantity and price.
  4. Check, then confirm. eTIMS creates the invoice and gives it a number.
  5. Send it to the customer: SMS, WhatsApp, email or print, depending on the channel you used.
  6. Record it in your tracker: Sales sheet → "eTIMS invoice issued?" = Yes, and type the invoice number.

Good practice

  • Make the invoice at the time of the sale, not at month end.
  • Make sure the amount matches what the customer paid on M-Pesa.
  • If you make a mistake, use the eTIMS option to correct it (a credit note). Don't just ignore it.

5. Getting eTIMS invoices from your suppliers

This is the habit that most directly protects you. Every business purchase without an eTIMS invoice is at risk of being rejected as a cost.

What to say: copy and send

EnglishKiswahili
"Hello, please send me an eTIMS invoice for today's purchase. My KRA PIN is ______. Thank you." "Habari, tafadhali nitumie invoice ya eTIMS ya mzigo wa leo. KRA PIN yangu ni ______. Asante."
"I can only pay against an eTIMS invoice. Can you generate it now?" "Nitalipa nikipata invoice ya eTIMS. Unaweza kuitengeneza sasa?"
"You can do it for free on *222# or the KRA eTIMS app." "Unaweza kuitengeneza bure kupitia *222# au app ya KRA eTIMS."

Make it automatic

  • Save your KRA PIN as a note on your phone so you can paste it quickly.
  • Prefer suppliers who issue eTIMS invoices without being asked. Over time, move your regular buying to them.
  • In the tracker, the Expenses sheet turns a row red when "eTIMS invoice received?" is not "Yes". Clear the red rows every week.
  • Ask a tax professional how to handle costs where an eTIMS invoice may not apply (for example some wages, bank charges or exempt items). Mark those as "Not needed" only once you are sure.

6. Which tax applies to you? A simple map

This map gives you the right questions to ask. It is not a ruling on your situation.

Your situation (commonly reported)Tax to ask KRA about
Resident small business, yearly sales roughly between KSh 1 million and 25 million Turnover Tax (TOT): a percentage of gross sales. It has been reported as 1.5%; confirm the current rate and how often you must pay.
Taxable sales of KSh 5 million or more in any 12 months VAT registration is required. A VAT-registered business cannot use Turnover Tax.
Yearly sales below about KSh 1 million, or you choose not to use TOT Income tax on your profit, declared in your annual return. Here, eTIMS-backed expenses matter most.
In your tracker, set Settings → Tax regime and Estimated tax rate. The Monthly Summary then shows a rough estimate so you can save the money in advance. It is only an estimate: KRA's systems (iTax / eCitizen) calculate what you actually owe.

Simple money rule

Every time money comes in, put a small percentage aside in a separate M-Pesa savings wallet or bank account for tax. Then tax day isn't a shock.

Check current tariffs and requirements with Safaricom before choosing.

Golden rules

  • Separate business and personal money. Personal transactions mixed in make your records useless.
  • Pay yourself a fixed amount and record it as Owner Drawings in the tracker. The sheet leaves drawings out of business costs.
  • Always record the M-Pesa transaction code (e.g. SKL4ABC123). It is your proof of payment.
  • Download your M-Pesa statement every week (in the M-Pesa app, or by requesting it through the M-Pesa menu or the Safaricom app) and paste it into the tracker's M-Pesa Check sheet.

Do this at closing time, every day. Kila siku, kabla ya kufunga.

Daily checklist

  • All sales recorded with payment method
  • All M-Pesa codes typed
  • All expenses recorded with eTIMS status
  • Credit sales added to Debtors
  • Cash counted and matches

9. Weekly and month-end checklists

Every week (15 minutes)

  • Download the M-Pesa statement and paste it into M-Pesa Check. Fix every orange "MISSING" row.
  • Go through red rows in Expenses and follow up with suppliers for eTIMS invoices.
  • Send polite reminders to OVERDUE debtors and record the date in "Last reminder sent".
  • Back up the file (download a copy or keep it in Google Drive).

Every month end (30 minutes)

  • Open Monthly Summary and look at sales, expenses and profit.
  • Check % of expenses covered by eTIMS. Aim for as close to 100% as possible.
  • Move the estimated tax amount into your tax savings.
  • File and pay any returns due on iTax / eCitizen by KRA's deadline. Confirm the dates on kra.go.ke.
  • Note your best-selling items and the costs you can cut next month.

10. Ten common mistakes

  1. Mixing personal and business M-Pesa. Use a Till, Paybill or Pochi for the business.
  2. Not asking suppliers for eTIMS invoices. Costs may be rejected and you pay more tax.
  3. Recording only M-Pesa sales. Cash sales count too.
  4. Forgetting credit sales. If it isn't written down, it often never gets collected.
  5. Waiting until month end to record. You will forget things. Do it daily.
  6. Counting all money received as profit. Profit = sales − costs. Check the Monthly Summary.
  7. Spending the tax money. Set it aside as you earn.
  8. Sharing your KRA password. Never send it to anyone by SMS or WhatsApp.
  9. Relying on social media "tax tips". Check with KRA or a professional.
  10. Not keeping a backup. Phones get lost. Keep the tracker in Google Drive.
